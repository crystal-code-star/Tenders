### 1.3 Why This Split Matters for Content

Posting the exact same content on both pages wastes the fact that you have two distinct angles on the same expertise.

**DO:**
- NT2E posts about projects, engineering challenges, completed installations, technical results, and sector trends (water stress, regulation, sustainability).
- Crystal Water posts about products, brands carried, stock availability, delivery, and practical maintenance tips.
- Both pages repost or comment on each other's content to reinforce the group relationship.

**DON'T:**
- NT2E posting product catalogue photos or price-driven content — undercuts its positioning as the technical expert.
- Crystal Water claiming full engineering/EPC credit for projects — that belongs to NT2E's narrative.
- Either page publishing contradictory information about who did what on a shared project.

## 2. What Makes a LinkedIn Post Good

### 2.1 The Anatomy of a High-Performing Post

LinkedIn's algorithm and human readers reward the same things: a reason to stop scrolling, a reason to keep reading, and a reason to engage. Every post should have four parts:

1. **Hook (first 1–2 lines).** Only this is visible before "see more." It must create curiosity, state a result, or ask a real question — never start with "We are pleased to announce..."
2. **Body (the story or substance).** Concrete details: numbers, capacities, a problem that was solved, a before/after. Short paragraphs (1–3 lines), line breaks between ideas — LinkedIn has no rich formatting, so whitespace does the work.
3. **Insight or takeaway.** Why should the reader care — what does this mean for their plant, their budget, their compliance, their water security?
4. **Call to action.** A specific next step: a question to the audience, an invite to comment, a link, or "message us to discuss your site." Avoid generic "Contact us today!"

### 2.2 Formatting Rules That Actually Move Engagement

- **Length:** 120–220 words is the sweet spot for industrial/B2B posts.
- **Line breaks:** Break after every 1–2 sentences. A wall of text gets scrolled past regardless of content quality.
- **First line:** Treat it like a headline — the only thing shown before "...more."
- **Hashtags:** 3–6 relevant hashtags at the end, not scattered through the text. Mix broad (#WaterTreatment) and specific (#OsmoseInverse, #ReverseOsmosis).
- **Emojis:** Use sparingly as visual markers (✅ 💧 ⚙️) to break up lists — never instead of substance.
- **Visuals:** Always attach an image, diagram, or short video. Posts with a relevant photo of equipment, the team on-site, or a simple diagram consistently outperform text-only posts in this industry.
- **Tagging:** Tag the client (if they allow it), partner brands, or technology partners mentioned.

### 2.3 Do's and Don'ts for an Industrial / Engineering Page

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Lead with the result: "10 m³/day of wastewater now reused for irrigation." | Lead with the company: "NT2E is proud to announce..." |
| Use real specs (capacity in m³/h or m³/j, EH, NTU, ppm) — this audience trusts numbers. | Use vague marketing language ("state-of-the-art," "world-class") with no data behind it. |
| Explain the *why*: water stress, cost savings, compliance, sustainability. | Only describe *what* was installed with no context on why it matters. |
| Show people: engineers on site, the install, the handover. | Only show stock product photos with no human or project context. |
| Post consistently (1–2x per week) even if simple. | Post only when there's a big announcement, then go silent for months. |

## 3. Content Pillars — What to Post About

A content pillar is a recurring theme you can return to indefinitely. Below are pillars built specifically from NT2E and Crystal Water's actual business lines, plus a new events pillar (detailed fully in Section 6).

### 3.1 NT2E Content Pillars

**Pillar A — Completed Project / Case Study**
The single best-performing content type for engineering companies.
- Include: client/sector (even anonymized — "a hotel in southern Morocco"), capacity (m³/h, m³/j, EH), technology used, the result in plain terms (water reused, money saved, compliance achieved).
- Good for: OCP, Pepsi/Varun, Air Liquide, Nexans, Coficab, Les Domaines Agricoles and other reference clients — always check what can be publicly shared first.

**Pillar B — Technology Explainers**
Short educational posts on one technology at a time: reverse osmosis, ultrafiltration, electrodeionization, MBBR, flocculation/coagulation.
- Format idea: "What is [technology] and when do you need it?" in under 150 words, plus a simple diagram.

**Pillar C — Sector Trends & Water Stress in Morocco**
Morocco's water stress, desalination investment, and industrial water reuse regulation are genuine, ongoing news topics.
- Format idea: a short take on a real development (a new desalination plant, a new regulation, a drought statistic) plus what it means for industrial clients.

**Pillar D — Behind the Scenes / Team & Culture**
Photos from site visits, commissioning days, the team at work, milestones (anniversaries, new hires, new certifications).

**Pillar E — Partner & Brand Spotlights**
NT2E works with named technology partners (e.g. AZUD, Delphin, Akim, Mega, Baltimore, Baysu, BIMAS/BIMAKS). A short post on why a specific membrane or technology partner was chosen for a project adds credibility by association.

**Pillar F — Events (see Section 6)**
Trade shows, conferences, and exhibitions — before, during, and after coverage.

### 3.2 Crystal Water Content Pillars

**Pillar A — Product Spotlight**
One product or product family per post: RO membranes, cooling tower drift eliminators, MBBR media, boiler treatment chemicals, the Mobile/Beta/Omega RO series, Seamar Watermakers.

**Pillar B — Maintenance Tips & "Did You Know"**
Practical, non-salesy advice: "How often should you replace your sand filter media?" "3 signs your cooling tower drift eliminator needs replacing."

**Pillar C — Stock, Logistics & Availability**
Crystal Water's positioning ("Achat simplifié," "Livraison rapide partout au Maroc") is itself a content angle.

**Pillar D — Brands Carried**
Spotlight the brands in the catalogue (BIMAKS and others) — what they make, why Crystal Water carries them, what problem they solve.

**Pillar E — Events (see Section 6)**
Booth presence, live demos, catalogue launches tied to trade shows.

## 5. Voice & Style Guide

### 5.1 Language

NT2E's existing posts are written primarily in French, which matches its B2B industrial Moroccan audience, with English technical terms used naturally ("Osmoflow," "AFM," brand/product names). **Recommendation:** keep French as the default for both pages, and use English only for international tenders, partners, or technical terms that don't translate well, and for posts specifically targeting international event attendees (e.g. foreign exhibitors at Pollutec or SIB).

### 5.2 Tone

- **NT2E:** Confident technical expert. Precise, factual, quietly proud of results rather than promotional. The existing tagline ("Quand écologique devient économique") is a good recurring closing line for project posts.
- **Crystal Water:** Helpful, practical, responsive. More "we make your life easier" than "we are the leading expert" — matching its own line "Cadrer vos besoins, vous faciliter l'industrie."

### 5.3 Recurring Hashtag Bank

## 6. EVENTS — The Complete Playbook

This section covers literally everything related to events: why they matter, how to plan content around them, what to post before/during/after, logistics, who should be involved, measurement, and a ready checklist. Treat this as the master reference any time NT2E or Crystal Water exhibits at, attends, sponsors, or even just follows a trade show, salon, or conference.

### 6.1 Why Events Matter for These Two Brands

For an industrial B2B company in a relationship-driven market like Morocco, events are not just marketing — they are where tenders get discussed informally, where technical partners are found, and where reference clients are met face-to-face. LinkedIn content tied to events does three things at once:
1. **Builds anticipation** — gets the right people (procurement engineers, plant managers, technical buyers) to plan a visit to your stand.
2. **Extends reach beyond the room** — most of your LinkedIn audience won't attend; event content is often their only exposure to it.
3. **Creates durable proof** — case-study and credibility content that outlives the event itself (photos, quotes, stats from panels).

### 6.2 Categories of Relevant Events

Not every event is the same; tailor the content strategy to the category.

| Event Type | Examples | Primary Content Angle |
|---|---|---|
| **Water / environment trade shows** | SITeau, Pollutec Maroc / Global Green Event | Technical demos, sector trend commentary, panel quotes |
| **Industrial / manufacturing trade shows** | SISTEP Industriel, IMME, SITEC-IMME | Equipment focus (Crystal Water lead), B2B networking |
| **Energy & sustainability events** | Solaire Expo Maroc, EneR Event, EMEC Expo | Sustainability angle, cross-promotion with renewable energy theme |
| **Construction / infrastructure** | Salon International du Bâtiment (SIB) | Water infrastructure for new developments, municipal angle |
| **Agriculture** | SIAM (Meknès) | Irrigation reuse, desalination for agriculture |
| **General B2B / multi-sector fairs** | Foire Internationale de Casablanca (FIC) | Brand visibility, broad audience |
| **Conferences & forums (no booth needed)** | Africa CEO Forum, water-policy conferences | Thought-leadership commentary, no stand required |
| **Internal/company milestones treated as events** | Anniversaries, plant inaugurations, certification ceremonies | Company culture & credibility content |

### 6.3 The Event Content Timeline — Before, During, After

Every event should generate **at least 3 posts per page involved** (more for major events like SITeau or Global Green Event). Spreading content across a timeline multiplies reach far more than one single "we were there" post after the fact.

#### Phase 1 — Before the Event (T-minus 2–4 weeks, then T-minus 1 week)

- **Save-the-date post** (2–4 weeks out): Announce attendance, stand number if known, what will be showcased. Use Template 4.5.
- **Teaser post** (about 1 week out): Tease one specific product, project, or topic that will be discussed. Good moment for a technology-explainer-style post tied to the event's theme.
- **"What to expect" post** (1–2 days out): A short list of what visitors can see/do at the stand — live demo, sample chemicals, brochure, technical team available for questions.
- **Internal action:** Confirm who from the team will be present, get their LinkedIn profiles ready to tag, prepare printed/digital materials, confirm photographer or phone-photo plan for the day.

#### Phase 2 — During the Event (each day it runs)

- **Day-one energy post:** Arrival, stand setup, first impressions. Use Template 4.6.
- **Mid-event highlight post:** A specific moment — a good conversation, a demo reaction, a stat or quote picked up from a conference session (always attribute correctly and keep quotes under 15 words if quoting a speaker).
- **Real-time photo/video posts:** Short, simple captions; let the visual carry the post. These perform well precisely because they feel unpolished and immediate.
- **Tag the organizer and co-exhibitors/partners** where appropriate — this often gets reshared, multiplying reach.
- **Internal action:** Assign one person per day as the "content person" on-site so posting doesn't get forgotten in the rush of meetings.

#### Phase 3 — After the Event (within 48 hours, then 1–2 weeks later)

- **Recap post** (within 48 hours, while still fresh): Outcome-first summary — what was learned, showcased, or discussed. Thank the organizer and visitors. Use Template 4.7.
- **Follow-up/lead-nurture post** (1–2 weeks later): A deeper dive into one specific topic that came up repeatedly in conversations at the booth — this turns hallway conversations into evergreen content.
- **Internal action:** Export/organize any contacts collected, follow up individually via LinkedIn or email within one week — the recap post is marketing, but personal follow-up closes the relationship.

### 6.4 Event Content Checklist (Master List)

**2–4 weeks before:**
- [ ] Confirm attendance, stand number, team attending
- [ ] Draft save-the-date post (Template 4.5)
- [ ] Identify 1–2 products/projects to spotlight at the event
- [ ] Prepare visuals: booth renders, product photos, past-event photos if reusing an annual event

**1 week before:**
- [ ] Publish teaser post
- [ ] Brief on-site team on what NOT to say publicly (pricing specifics, unannounced client names, unconfirmed project details)
- [ ] Assign daily "content person" for multi-day events
- [ ] Charge phones/cameras, test photo quality in advance

**Each day of the event:**
- [ ] At least 1 photo or video post
- [ ] Tag organizer, partners, and any consenting visitors/clients
- [ ] Use the event's official hashtag (check their LinkedIn page or website for it)
- [ ] Monitor and reply to comments same-day — events generate real-time engagement that fades fast

**Within 48 hours after:**
- [ ] Publish recap post (Template 4.7)
- [ ] Thank organizer and key visitors/partners by name (with permission)
- [ ] Collect and organize any business cards / contacts gathered

**1–2 weeks after:**
- [ ] Publish a deeper-dive follow-up post on a topic that resonated at the booth
- [ ] Personally follow up with key contacts (not just via public post)
- [ ] Add the event's outcome/lessons to next year's planning notes

### 6.5 Who Should Be Involved

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Content owner (marketing/comms)** | Drafts and schedules before/after posts; coordinates with on-site team |
| **On-site "content person" (rotating)** | Takes photos/videos, posts in real time, captures quotes |
| **Technical lead/engineer present** | Provides accurate technical detail for captions (capacities, technologies, specs) — avoids embarrassing factual errors |
| **Management/founders** | Personal LinkedIn posts/reshares from individual accounts significantly outperform company-page-only posts; encourage at least one leadership post per event |
| **Sales/commercial team** | Owns the post-event personal follow-up with contacts collected |

### 6.6 Event-Specific Do's and Don'ts

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Get explicit permission before naming or photographing visiting clients/partners | Post a photo with a recognizable competitor's logo or a client who hasn't agreed to be named |
| Use the event's own official hashtag — it gets you discovered by the organizer's network too | Invent a hashtag no one else is using for the event |
| Mix professional (booth, demos) and human (team laughing, setting up) content | Post only stiff, posed "executive handshake" photos |
| Credit conference speakers accurately if quoting their stats or remarks | Misquote or paraphrase a stat in a way that distorts what was actually said |
| Treat the recap post as a thank-you to the *community*, not a victory lap | Make the recap entirely self-congratulatory with no value for the reader who wasn't there |

### 6.7 Repurposing Event Content Later

Event content has a long shelf life if you treat it as raw material, not a one-time post:
- **Photos** from a stand or demo can become the visual for a later Technology Explainer post.
- **A conversation theme** that came up repeatedly at a booth ("everyone kept asking about ZLD") can become its own dedicated explainer post weeks later.
- **A quote or stat from a conference panel** can anchor a Sector Trends post even months later, properly attributed.
- **Year-over-year comparisons** ("Last year at SITeau we showcased X — this year, Y") are a strong way to show growth and continuity.

### 6.8 Internal Events Worth Treating the Same Way

Not every "event" is external. The same before/during/after content logic applies to:
- Plant/installation inaugurations or handovers
- Company anniversaries (NT2E's 12+ years is a natural milestone series)
- New certifications or partnership signings
- Internal training days or safety milestones
- New hire announcements tied to team growth

## 9. Final Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] Does the first line work as a stand-alone hook (no "see more" needed to understand the value)?
- [ ] Is there at least one concrete number or specific detail (capacity, result, timeframe)?
- [ ] Are paragraphs broken into 1–2 line chunks with white space between them?
- [ ] Is there an image, diagram, or video attached?
- [ ] Is the right page posting it — NT2E for projects/engineering, Crystal Water for products/parts?
- [ ] Is client/partner permission confirmed before naming them or posting site/event photos?
- [ ] Are there 3–6 relevant hashtags at the end, not mid-text?
- [ ] Is there a clear call to action (question, comment prompt, or contact instruction)?
- [ ] **If event-related:** is this post correctly placed in the before/during/after timeline (Section 6.3), and is the official event hashtag used?

> **Quick reminder:** When in doubt about a post, ask: "Would a plant manager or procurement engineer actually stop scrolling for this?" If the honest answer is no, the hook needs work — not the whole post.
